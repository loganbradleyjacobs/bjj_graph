/goal The goal is to finalize the data storage method used for this project. I currently use one moveset.json file to list all of the moves. I render this in a graph, with nodes and edges, where the positions are the nodes, and the edges would be the moves, if they were named, which they are currently not. The edges are generated from the parent to the child. This might even be a misnomer though, as I want the edges to be bidirectional, like they are rendered. Should I use an adjacency graph or an adjacency list, keep the json representation, or go to a nosql database?

/goal I want to revisit how we are representing the data. My qualm with storing submissions as edges is that they are not simple moves, they often have smaller details that need to be adjusted for the submission to work, and I want to represent my knowledge of those too. I am okay with storing a md file or imgs/video links along with the node to walk me through this, but I don't want to do all that for edges. Submissions should be a leaf node, and often are in my graph, I just noticed for example, Clock Choke is a self-loop, but also Clock Choke from Back is a submission node, so I wanted to be clear on that.

/goal I want to revisit the way that nodes are displayed. I am unhappy with the current look. Edges are arrows (I thought they were rendered bidirectionally, like I would have wanted, sorry), but the main issue is the density of edges and nodes. Edges run every which direction. I know that relatively dense graphs are hard to visualize, but I want to do better than this. Perhaps the edges have weight to them based on the type of them? Like a position like armbar should be closer to spiderweb than closed-guard, for example, but probably equidistant from both closed-guard and mount. escapes probably have a low pull on their connecting nodes, because they are less likely. I don't know much about visualizing a graph like this, but that seems like a decent place to start, let me know if not.
